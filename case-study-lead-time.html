Automating Packaging Reorder Alerts

From a vague operational complaint to a transparent decision system that makes packaging risk visible, consistent, and actionable.

At a glance

Turn a risky manual judgment into a repeatable rule.

Packaging stock, future production needs, pending purchase orders, and supplier delays lived in different places. Knowing whether a shortage was actually coming relied on one person correctly combining four moving numbers under pressure, every time.

Role
Sole owner: problem discovery, decision-logic design, build, and rollout
Scope
End-to-end project
Key outcome
Replaced ad hoc, item-by-item review with a rule-based system covering every tracked packaging item each cycle.

Discovery

Find the actual failure beneath the symptom.

The operations team did not arrive with a clean specification. They had a recurring pattern: packaging came dangerously close to running out, and by the time it was obvious there was not always enough runway left to act. Walking through the workflow revealed four individually-correct inputs that were never consistently combined: daily stock movement, production plans, pending purchase orders, and supplier lead times.

Reframing the ask

The initial ask was for a single dashboard. But a dashboard would improve visibility without removing the difficult judgment call. The product question became: how might we convert a high-pressure, manual calculation into a rule the system applies consistently?

Decision

Choose the approach with the least friction.

Considered

Unified dashboard

Value: Places all four data sources on one screen.

Trade-off: Still leaves the manual urgency judgment with the team.

Considered

Standalone inventory app

Value: Provides full control over the data model and interface.

Trade-off: Creates switching cost for a team already fluent in Sheets.

Chosen direction

Rule-based decision layer

Value: Automates the judgment while preserving the team's existing workflow.

Trade-off: Less visual novelty, but near-zero adoption friction.

Framework

Turn one fuzzy question into four clear checks.

01

Calculate effective stock

Evaluate physical stock and incoming purchase orders together, not in isolation.

02

Protect the minimum floor

Apply the business's agreed minimum-stock threshold to every item, every cycle.

03

Account for lead-time demand

Check whether stock can cover expected consumption while a new supplier order arrives.

04

Separate trigger from quantity

Return both a clear status and a suggested order quantity, rather than one blended judgment.

Operating model

Keep the source transparent. Automate the judgment.

  1. 01

    Google Sheets remains the transparent source of truth for stock, production demand, pending orders, lead times, thresholds, and calculations.

  2. 02

    Google Apps Script evaluates each packaging item against the decision framework automatically.

  3. 03

    Categorized alerts communicate both the status and the numbers behind it: Critical, Reorder Now, or Safe.

  4. 04

    The team can audit the logic without needing to inspect a hidden technical system.

Impact

Make routine checks quieter and exceptions clearer.

+

100% of tracked packaging items are evaluated every cycle instead of relying on occasional spot checks.

+

Detection moves from the next time someone reviews a sheet to same-cycle automatic alerting.

+

The system structurally prevents unnecessary reorders when stock is already incoming.

+

Routine review time is substantially reduced, while human attention is reserved for real exceptions.

Next

Extend only after the foundation is trusted.

The team asked for a better way to see a hard decision. The more valuable product move was to remove the need to make that decision manually in the first place.
